# Meeting with Prof. Mohamed Abdelrazek, 2026-10-14

Sagandeep Kaur and Prof. Mohamed Abdelrazek (Deakin A2I2). Written up on 2026-10-15 from six short notes taken in the meeting. The notes are quoted as written. The readings below are mine. Where a note could mean two things, both readings are given, and the one the plan uses for now is marked **to confirm with Prof. Abdelrazek**.

The resulting changes to the plan are listed under "Amendments 2026-10-15" at the top of [TECHNICAL_PLAN.md](TECHNICAL_PLAN.md).

---

### 1. "baseline cost : task"

- **Reading.** Report the cost per task for each baseline, not only success rates.
- **In the plan.** Section 4.2 already matches baselines on mean cost per held-out task, and section 7.2 lists cost per task. What was missing: medians, wall-clock time, pass rate per dollar, and a dollar figure for local models (item 6).
- **Action.** New section 4.3, "Cost per task reporting". In code, `evaluation/runner.py` reports only mean calls, tokens and dollars per task; it needs medians, wall-clock time and pass rate per dollar. `baselines/cost.py` needs a GPU-hour rate next to the token price table.

### 2. "benchmark : task"

- **Reading.** Be explicit about which benchmark is used and which of its tasks go where: the task list, the split, and results reported per task.
- **In the plan.** Section 2 (benchmark choice) and section 7.1 (splits). [PHASE0_CHECKLIST.md](PHASE0_CHECKLIST.md) proposes pins, but the 89 Terminal-Bench 2.0 task ids have not been extracted, so no split file exists.
- **Action.** No change to the plan. Done on 2026-10-15: the 89 task ids with category and difficulty are in `evaluation/data/tb2_tasks.jsonl` and the seeded split is `splits/terminal-bench-2.0.json`. Add a per-task pass/fail table to the final report.

### 3. "strategy adapt"

- **Reading.** Strategy adaptation, the first-order loop, is the core of the project. This also matches the plan's choice of "strategy adaptation" over "steering".
- **In the plan.** Section 5. `adaptation_engine/first_order.py` has the round's interface with rule-based stand-ins.
- **Action.** No change. The LLM diagnoser and proposer are Phase 2 work, as planned.

### 4. "intelligence index"

- **Reading (a), adopted for now, to confirm with Prof. Abdelrazek.** Chollet's definition of intelligence as "skill-acquisition efficiency" (F. Chollet, *On the Measure of Intelligence*, arXiv 1911.01547, Nov 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01547). For this project: one number for held-out gain per unit of adaptation cost.
- **Reading (b).** A composite score over several benchmarks, like the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index. Under this reading the index is mostly a way to compare candidate models, which belongs with the model choice in [SMALL_MODELS.md](SMALL_MODELS.md).
- **In the plan.** Nothing under either reading. Section 6.2 scores the second-order loop by improvement per cost, which is close to (a).
- **Action.** New section 7.6, "Adaptation efficiency index", and open decision 13. Chollet (2019) is not yet in [PRIOR_WORK.md](PRIOR_WORK.md); add it to section 3 there.

### 5. "strategy switching"

- **Reading (a), adopted for now, to confirm with Prof. Abdelrazek.** Keep a small library of strategy versions and a selector that picks one per task, from task features or the results of early probes. This is compared with the plan's single global strategy that is edited over time.
- **Reading (b).** Switch to a different strategy in the middle of a task when progress stalls.
- **In the plan.** Section 5 has a single global strategy only. Reading (b) is close to the existing recovery field `on_repeated_failure` (`replan`, `restart_task`); it could become one more value of that field.
- **Action.** New section 5.4, "Strategy switching", as an extra condition, and open decision 14. The selector's own cost is counted in the per-task cost.

### 6. "small models on terminal bench, coz everyone can run small models on local machine"

- **Reading.** Use small open-weight models, served locally, as the agent's model on Terminal-Bench. Anyone can then rerun the experiments without paying for an API.
- **In the plan.** This replaces GPT-5-mini as the first candidate (section 2, open decision 5). The 20–40% starting-score requirement stays. The plan already notes that the smallest models sit near the floor on Terminal-Bench, so this is the main risk of the change. Dollars for local runs become compute hours times a GPU rate (section 4.3). The budget in section 9 assumed API prices and has to be re-estimated.
- **Action.** Candidates and their published scores are collected in [SMALL_MODELS.md](SMALL_MODELS.md). The Phase 0 starting-score check now runs on those candidates first. GPT-5-mini is the fallback if none of them reaches about 20%. `llm/` needs a client for a locally served model. Who provides the GPU is part of open decision 2.
